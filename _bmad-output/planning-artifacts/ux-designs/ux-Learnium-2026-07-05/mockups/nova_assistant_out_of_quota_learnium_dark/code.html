System Design for Scale

When engineering systems that must handle millions of concurrent operations, vertical scaling (adding more power to a single machine) quickly hits physical and financial ceilings. The architectural pivot to horizontal scaling involves distributing the load across commodity hardware.

Database Sharding

Sharding involves partitioning your database into smaller, faster, more easily managed parts called data shards. The word shard means a small part of a whole.

However, distributed architectures introduce new complexities, particularly around state management and data consistency. The CAP theorem dictates that in the presence of a network partition, one has to choose between consistency and availability.

Think of database sharding like splitting a massive library into specialized branches across a city. Instead of everyone crowding into the Central Library to find a book (bottleneck!), fiction goes to the West branch, history to the East branch.

It speeds up queries and distributes the load, but the brutal reality is that cross-shard joins become a logistical nightmare.
